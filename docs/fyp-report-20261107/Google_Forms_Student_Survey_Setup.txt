GOOGLE FORMS — STUDENT CUSTOMER-USABILITY SURVEY

Create at https://forms.google.com (or https://forms.new). The Word/PDF files are editable/printable instruments, not a hosted Google Form.

Title: Customer Usability Evaluation — Wrap & Bowl Kuch
Description: This voluntary academic survey evaluates a home-kitchen preorder website. Please try the assigned customer tasks first. Use the researcher’s test contact details and stop before actual payment or order placement. Do not upload a real bank receipt. Responses are used for this FYP; do not enter your name, phone or email in this questionnaire.

Before sharing: confirm consent/recruitment with your supervisor; preview on a phone; use a supervised test setup; keep the form closed until reviewed.

SETTINGS
- Turn off email collection and any organisation-only restriction if your approved survey is anonymous/public.
- Requiring one response usually requires Google sign-in; use only if appropriate for your recruitment plan.
- Do not shuffle sections/questions: the task instructions and consent must stay first.
- When ready, publish/enable responses and use the responder link, not the edit link. Current Google Forms may call this Publish; older layouts use Send → link.
- Link Responses to Google Sheets for analysis if desired.

SECTION 1 — Consent and context
Consent (required, multiple choice): I have read the purpose and voluntarily agree / I do not agree. Route “I do not agree” to Submit/end.
Device (multiple choice): Phone / Tablet / Laptop or desktop / Other.
Experience ordering food online (multiple choice): Never / Occasionally / Frequently.
Passkey access (multiple choice): Supported and tried / Not supported / Not attempted.

SECTION 2 — Tasks
Use an offered date and the agreed delivery area. Select three selling units within a feasible whole-order budget, such as RM60. A meal, box or pack is one unit. Stop before actual payment/order placement.
Task A: ordinary menu browsing. Task B: date suggestions. The researcher assigns/counterbalances A-first and B-first to reduce learning effects.
Record task success, assistance and measured time separately if conducting the comparison. Do not call self-reported time a stopwatch measurement.

SECTION 3 — Ratings
Multiple-choice grid rows below; columns: Strongly disagree / Disagree / Neither agree nor disagree / Agree / Strongly agree / Not tried or not applicable.
S1: I could identify which kitchen I was ordering from.
S2: The menu price and the meaning of a meal, box or pack were clear.
S3: I could find an offered date and an allowed delivery/collection time.
S4: I could see the delivery fee and complete order total before placing an order.
S5: I understood that uploading a receipt does not mean the kitchen has verified payment.
S6: The customer account and passkey sign-in instructions were understandable.
S7: I could return from a dialog or recover from an input error without losing my place.
S8: The suggestions were relevant to the date, quantity and preferences in my task.
S9: I understood the difference between Allow a mix and One menu only.
S10: I could tell whether the suggested complete basket was within or above my budget.

S11 (multiple choice): Compared with ordinary browsing, the suggestions required: Much less effort / Less effort / About the same / More effort / Much more effort / Not compared.
S12 (multiple choice): If the real menu, price and date suited you, how likely would you be to preorder? Very unlikely / Unlikely / Unsure / Likely / Very likely. This measures intention, not real purchase conversion.
S13 (multiple choice): Do recorded batch dates alone guarantee that food is physically fresh and safe? Yes / No / Not sure. Do not show the correct answer before the response.

S14: Which screen or step was most confusing, and why? (optional paragraph)
S15: What would you change to make ordering easier? (optional paragraph)
S16: Was there anything you could not test, including passkey/device access? (optional paragraph)

OBJECTIVE MAP FOR YOUR REPORT (not a participant rating of hidden staff functions)
O1: S3–S7 and observed customer task success. O3: S1–S2 and customer comments. O5: S8–S12 plus matched task measurements where conducted. S13 only tests comprehension of the public freshness claim; it does not validate actual inventory calculations. The client should evaluate O2/O4 staff workflows.

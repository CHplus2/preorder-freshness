00:00.00–00:05.44 | From the menu to the kitchen
Here's how a preorder moves from the menu to the kitchen.

00:05.44–00:17.16 | Browse the kitchen menu
Let's start with the menu. Customers can see prices and portions, then add a meal. They can review their basket before signing in.

00:17.16–00:29.28 | Create an account with a passkey
At checkout, customers enter their name and phone number, then create a passkey. They don't need a separate website password or email address.

00:29.28–00:43.08 | Find ideas for a date and budget
Want some ideas? Choose a date, quantity and budget. The finder suggests complete options, including one menu repeated. Customers can compare the choices before ordering.

00:43.08–01:06.60 | Confirm the date, total and receipt
At checkout, choose an offered date, a morning time and the delivery area. The total is confirmed before the kitchen's receiving QR appears. A sample receipt is uploaded here. The kitchen still needs to verify the payment. The saved order is then shown in My orders.

01:06.60–01:16.76 | Review payment separately
The kitchen can open the order and review its payment evidence. Recording payment and confirming that the food was received are separate actions.

01:16.76–01:30.40 | Ingredients and packaging update together
Stock updates when the order is placed. In this example, chicken goes from five thousand to four thousand eight hundred and seventy grams. One salad bowl is also deducted.

01:30.40–01:44.24 | Record batch dates and storage life
For unlabelled ingredients, the owner can record shelf life and its reference. Earlier limits and held batches affect usable stock. These dates help with planning; they don't guarantee food safety.

01:44.24–02:00.00 | Print daily orders and review sales
Finally, the kitchen can review the day's orders and print the matching slips together. Sales and costs follow their recorded payment and receipt rules. The order stays connected to the kitchen's records.

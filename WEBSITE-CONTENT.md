# Business website structure and content brief

Adapted from the supplied AI Business Design Hackathon prompt. The goal is a clear five-page customer journey, rather than a catalogue with generic filler.

## Confirmed

- Single-vendor storefront for a Malaysian home-based food business.
- Flexible food preorders, ingredient batch tracking, owner planning, sales analytics and menu recommendations.
- Main action: explore the menu, add food to a basket and choose a delivery date/time.
- Dapur Kita is the current working brand, not a confirmed real beneficiary name.

## Customer journey

1. **Home — discover and understand:** identify the home kitchen offering immediately; use occasion tabs, a current-menu preview, owner introduction and a clear menu CTA.
2. **Our Story — build trust:** real owner name and portrait, why they started, their journey, approach to food and signature dish. These must come from the beneficiary.
3. **Menu — explore and choose:** live catalogue, search, categories, prices, notice periods and portion capacity; individual pages include ingredients status and verified-order reviews.
4. **How It Works — remove uncertainty:** three-step ordering guide, preparation timing explanation, delivery expectations and interactive FAQs.
5. **Contact — take action:** confirmed service area, email, WhatsApp and social media, with a topic/message builder that opens a messaging-app draft. It never pretends a message was sent.

The header and footer connect all five pages. Desktop navigation becomes a mobile menu. A basket count, account menu, focus states and reduced-motion support help people complete the task comfortably.

## Brand direction

Recommended direction: warm, thoughtful, personal and easy to use. Forest green, warm cream and terracotta; expressive serif headings with readable sans-serif body text. Use restrained entrance animations and hover feedback. The hero’s abstract typography is a design illustration, not a depiction of a real product or kitchen.

Primary CTA: Explore the menu. Secondary CTA: Read our story. Supporting actions: contact the owner, view an item and follow an order. No invented testimonials, awards, customer counts or guarantees.

## Information required before a real-business launch

All unprovided facts are **[TO BE CONFIRMED WITH BENEFICIARY]**:

- Actual trading name and preferred brand identity.
- Owner’s name, short introduction and permission to use their portrait.
- Why the business started; meaningful events in its journey, in the owner’s own words.
- Signature food and genuine reasons customers choose it.
- Actual products, current prices, photographs and descriptions (the existing grocery catalogue has been preserved).
- Confirmed delivery/service area, contact email, WhatsApp number and social links.
- Any collection arrangements, business hours or policies that should be published.
- Allergen handling, cancellation/refund rules and real payment arrangements.

Owner-editable fields are available in Brand & settings. Missing biography and contact facts are visibly unfilled, rather than fabricated.

## Photography

Prefer real photographs of the owner, their actual menu items, preparation process and packaging. Use landscape food images for cards and portrait framing for the owner story. Obtain permission for identifiable people. AI images may support abstract decoration, but must not be presented as actual staff, dishes or customers.

## Acceptance checks

- All five pages and navigation links work, including direct URLs.
- The first screen explains the offering and gives a clear next step.
- Actual biography and contact details are supplied before launch.
- Menu content reflects what the kitchen really sells.
- CTA, basket, authentication and checkout work on the deployed origin.
- Mobile menu, FAQs, occasion tabs and enquiry drafts respond correctly.
- Reduced-motion preferences disable decorative animation.
- No secrets appear in source, compiled assets or Git history.
- No fabricated claims, reviews, certifications or promised sales increases.
